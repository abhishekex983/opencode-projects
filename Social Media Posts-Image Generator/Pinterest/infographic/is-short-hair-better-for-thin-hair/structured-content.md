## Module 2 — WHY SHORT WINS (1×2 bento)

**Visual**: Two minimal line icons side by side:
- Left icon: long hair with downward arrow (label "WEIGHT PULLS DOWN")
- Right icon: short hair with upward arrow (label "ROOTS LIFT")

**Headline**: WHY SHORT WINS

**Content** (verbatim from blog):
- Long hair is heavy — that weight pulls strands downward from the root.
- Short cuts remove that excess weight, allowing roots to lift and create natural volume.
- Long ends go wispy and almost transparent, emphasizing lack of density.
- A shorter, blunter cut creates a solid, defined line — making hair look much thicker.

**Visual**: 4 line icons in a 2×2 grid, one per cut:
- Crew Cut: short-cropped top with uniform length silhouette
- Textured Crop: short sides with textured top line icon
- Buzz Cut: uniform ultra-short silhouette
- Fade: gradient sides silhouette with darker top

**Headline**: 4 BEST MEN'S CUTS

**Cell content** (one per icon):
1. **CREW CUT** — Classic, clean, uniform length minimizes contrast between thicker and thinner areas.
2. **TEXTURED CROP** — Short sides + textured top adds movement and hides sparse spots.
3. **BUZZ CUT** — Lowest maintenance; even length across the whole head masks thinning zones.
4. **FADE** — Gradient sides draw the eye upward, making the top appear more dense and full of texture.

## Module 4 — STYLING SECRETS (1×2 bento)

**Visual**: Numbered line icons (1–4) — blow-dryer + round brush + texture spray bottle + matte product jar

**Headline**: STYLING SECRETS

**Content** (adapted from blog, men-focused):
1. Blow-dry with a round brush, lifting at the roots to build body.
2. Flip head upside down while drying for extra lift.
3. Mist with texture spray — adds grit and fullness to fine strands.
4. Finish with a matte product for hold without the weighted, greasy look.

---

## Module 5 — MISTAKES TO AVOID (2×1 bento, RED ACCENT MODULE)

**Visual**: 3 warning line icons in red (#b91c1c): scissors-on-layered-hair + razor + sparse-ends

**Headline**: MISTAKES TO AVOID

**Content** (verbatim):
- Over-layering — removes too much bulk; leaves sparse patches + see-through ends.
- Razor cuts — shred fine ends, look frayed and thinner.
- Wispy/thin ends — spotlight sparse areas instead of building density.

**Use blunt, sharp lines** for the strongest visual foundation of density.

## Module 6 — PRO TIP: THE FADE (1×2 bento, KEY MEN'S TIP)

**Visual**: Line icon — head silhouette with gradient sides + arrow pointing UP from sides to top

**Headline**: PRO TIP — THE FADE

**Cell content**:
- Ask your barber for a subtle fade on the sides.
- Fade minimizes contrast between thicker and thinner areas.
- Draws the eye upward → makes the hair on top appear more dense and full of texture.
- Strategic approach hides gaps or bald spots an observer might otherwise see.
- Creates a clean, confident look that works WITH your hair, not against it.
