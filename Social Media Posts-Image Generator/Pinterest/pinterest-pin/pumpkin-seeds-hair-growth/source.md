Eating pumpkin seeds for hair growth provides your body with zinc, beta-sitosterol, omega-6 fatty acids, and antioxidants — nutrients that research suggests support the hair growth cycle. ## What's Inside Pumpkin Seeds That Supports Hair Growth

Pumpkin seeds pack a surprising amount of hair-healthy nutrients into a small serving. Understanding what's inside them helps explain why they've become a staple in my diet.

**Zinc** is one of the most important minerals for hair health. Pumpkin seeds contain approximately 7.5 mg of zinc per 100 grams. Zinc plays a role in protein synthesis and cell division — both of which are necessary for hair growth. Research suggests that zinc deficiency can contribute to hair loss, and getting enough from food sources like pumpkin seeds is a practical way to cover your bases.

**Beta-sitosterol** is a plant sterol found in pumpkin seeds, with approximately 265 mg of phytosterols per 100 grams. Beta-sitosterol may influence the enzyme 5-alpha-reductase, which is involved in the conversion of testosterone to DHT.

**Omega-6 fatty acids**, particularly linoleic acid, are another component of pumpkin seeds. Omega-6 and omega-3 fatty acids support overall scalp health and may help reduce inflammation around hair follicles.

**Vitamin E and antioxidants** round out the nutritional profile. Vitamin E acts as an antioxidant that helps protect cells from oxidative stress.

One clinical trial found that participants taking 400 mg of pumpkin seed oil daily for 24 weeks saw a 40% increase in hair count, while the placebo group only saw a 10% increase. Researchers also noted that self-rated improvement and satisfaction were significantly higher in the pumpkin seed oil group at 24 weeks.

## How to Eat Pumpkin Seeds for Hair Growth

**As a snack.** The simplest approach is just eating them straight. I keep a bag of pumpkin seeds on hand and grab a handful whenever I'm having barley tea, coffee, or chai.

**In oatmeal.** I mix pumpkin seeds into my morning oats along with walnuts and blueberries. The combination is genuinely delicious — the seeds add a nice crunch, and the walnuts and berries round out the flavor.

**Raw vs roasted.** Raw pumpkin seeds are much more nutritious than roasted pumpkin seeds, mainly because they're in their unprocessed form. Roasting can reduce some of the delicate fatty acids and antioxidants.

**How much to eat.** There's no specific dosage I follow — I just eat a handful whenever I feel like it. Research suggests that the benefits come from consistent, daily intake rather than occasional large amounts.
