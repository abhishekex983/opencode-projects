## Rosemary Oil as a Natural Alternative to Minoxidil

That's when I discovered rosemary oil. It's often described as a natural alternative to minoxidil, and honestly, I was surprised.

I leaned into a natural routine using rosemary oil, jojoba oil, and derma rolling once a week with a 0.5mm needle. I was hopeful. I massage it into my scalp, focusing on the areas where I was losing hair, and stuck with it for about a year.

The results? It helped me maintain the hair I had. But it wasn't anything dramatic — it couldn't stop the baldness or deliver real regrowth.

Eventually, a bald spot appeared and my hair loss sped up, which was honestly a little scary.

Here's what research I came across suggests: A 2015 study by Panahi and colleagues compared rosemary oil to 2% minoxidil in 100 men with androgenetic alopecia. Both groups showed a significant increase in hair count after 6 months. There was no significant difference between the two groups — meaning rosemary oil performed similarly to minoxidil 2% in that study. One interesting finding was that scalp itching was more frequent in the minoxidil group compared to the rosemary oil group.

From what I've read, rosemary oil also has some interesting properties that researchers are exploring. One study I found suggested it may have 5-alpha-reductase inhibitory activity — which is something that comes up frequently in hair loss research. However, this isn't medical advice, and individual results vary. Your dermatologist can tell you if rosemary oil might be worth considering for your specific situation.

Some research I've seen indicates that <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25842469" target="_blank" rel="nofollow">rosemary oil showed comparable results to minoxidil 2%</a> in the treatment of androgenetic alopecia. While research suggests promise, more studies are needed to fully understand the long-term effects.

## Other Natural Alternatives I Tried

Rosemary oil wasn't the only natural option I explored. When I was searching for a minoxidil alternative, I tried several things:

**Jojoba oil** — I mixed this with rosemary oil and applied it to my scalp. It felt good on the skin, and I liked the routine of massaging it in. Whether it made a real difference for my hair, I'm not entirely sure.

**Derma rolling** — I used a 0.5mm needle roller once a week to stimulate my scalp. The idea was to boost blood flow and help the oils absorb better. I stuck with this routine for about a year alongside the oils.

**Pumpkin seed oil** — I tried this as a DHT blocker, both applying it topically and eating pumpkin seeds regularly. It's another natural option that comes up frequently in hair loss discussions.

## What Eventually Worked for Me

After about a year of using rosemary oil, jojoba oil, and derma rolling, I had to face reality. The natural routine helped me maintain what I had, but it couldn't stop the progression of my hair loss. A bald spot showed up, and my hair loss accelerated.

That's when I decided to try something different. I'd always known about minoxidil and finasteride, but only the oral forms. I finally learned about topical gels and creams, which are supposed to be safer. After some research, I found a topical gel and decided to give it a shot.

I've been using it for almost a year now and, to be honest, it's been working pretty well. For now, I'm sticking with this topical approach, but I might team it up with a derma roller down the line.
