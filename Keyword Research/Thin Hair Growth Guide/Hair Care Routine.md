I first started noticing my hair thinning back in **2018**. Up until then, I always had pretty thick hair, so seeing it get thinner was tough. I know it’s pretty normal for hair to thin out in your late twenties, but I just couldn’t sit by and watch it happen without at least giving something a try. That’s when I started using **olive oil** every other day. After three months, my hair actually felt better—thicker, and the texture improved a bit. It wasn’t a miracle cure or anything, but I was fairly happy with the results for a while.

After a few months, I wanted to step things up. I saw a reel on **Instagram** about **pumpkin seed oil** and decided to do some digging. Turns out, pumpkin seed oil is a natural DHT blocker, which is great for hair health, so I started mixing it with olive oil. I was super intentional about how I applied it—really massaging it in with my fingertips to boost blood flow to my scalp, not just slapping it on.

I also tried out a **DHT blocking shampoo** with apple cider vinegar. It smelled great, didn’t lather much, and actually left my hair looking shinier and healthier.

Then there was the **onion juice** experiment. I applied it straight to my scalp, and—don’t judge me—even drank a little every morning after waking up. The idea was to improve scalp blood flow and hopefully encourage hair growth. So, I basically threw the kitchen sink at my hair: onion juice, pumpkin seed oil, olive oil… anything that might help.

But in **2021**, things got more real. I started seeing actual gaps in my hair and began worrying about **male pattern baldness**. I read about **minoxidil** and **finasteride**, but all the potential side effects scared me off. Instead, I leaned into more natural options—**rosemary oil**, **jojoba oil**, and eventually **derma rolling**. I stuck with that routine for about a year and saw some improvement, but it wasn’t anything dramatic.

The derma roller itself was a whole journey. Finding the right one was harder than I thought—I ended up with a $25 roller from Amazon. Picking the right needle length was tricky too, but I settled on 0.5mm since that’s supposed to be just enough to stimulate the scalp and boost blood flow without going overboard. I’d use it once a week to give my scalp time to heal and let my hair hopefully regrow.

Then, in **2025**, I met this guy while commuting who recommended his girlfriend—a hair stylist. I ended up booking an appointment with her, and she asked all about my diet, then suggested hair cream, a serum, and some supplements with **iron, biotin, and vitamin C**. I gave everything a shot for a couple of months, but honestly, I didn’t see much difference. Deep down, I was starting to accept that it was probably just male pattern baldness, even though she said my diet could be to blame.

That was my breaking point—I decided to try something totally new. I’d always known about minoxidil and finasteride, but only the oral forms. I finally learned about topical gels and creams, which are supposed to be safer. After some research, I found **Xyon hair gel** and decided to give it a shot. At first, I used gloves to apply it (I was still worried about side effects), but that got old fast, so I started just using my hands.

Eventually, a bald spot showed up and my hair loss sped up, which was honestly a little scary. Even though things like rosemary oil and derma rolling had helped me maintain what hair I had, they just couldn’t stop the baldness. I did more research and realized minoxidil mainly helps with blood flow—it won’t regrow hair by itself. For real regrowth, you need something like finasteride too.

After a lot of hesitation, I finally tried **Xyon gel**—a topical that feels a lot safer to me than taking pills. I’ve been using it for almost a year now and, to be honest, it’s been working pretty well. For now, I’m sticking with this topical approach, but I might team it up with a derma roller down the line. Who knows? I’ll see how it goes\!
